# The Blueprint: How Divinity: Original Sin 2 Became the Foundation of Modern CRPGs

**Slug:** `divinity-original-sin-2-blueprint`

---

Ghent, Belgium. 2015. Forty people in a studio that had spent years flirting with bankruptcy. Larian had bet everything on a Kickstarter for a sequel to a game that — to be candid — most people had managed not to play. The goal was half a million dollars. Modest by industry standards; audacious given what they intended to build. Swen Vincke, the CEO in the now-famous armour, stood before a camera and asked the internet to fund the most reactive RPG ever conceived. A game where you could kill anyone. A game where the story would bend around your choices the way water bends around a stone — or, more to the point, the way a budget bends around a stretch goal.

They hit the goal in twelve hours.

By the time the campaign closed, 42,713 backers had pledged $2,032,434 — four times the target [Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/larianstudios/divinity-original-sin-2). Every stretch goal was unlocked [WCCFTech](https://wccftech.com/divinity-original-sin-2-got-2m-from-kickstarter-all-stretch-goals-unlocked/). It was the 15th most-funded video game on Kickstarter at the time. And nobody — not the backers, not the press, not even Larian themselves — fully understood what they had just set in motion. That is the nature of revolutions: no one recognises them at the time, least of all the revolutionaries.

Two years later, Divinity: Original Sin 2 sold 500,000 copies in its first four days [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/71036-divinity-original-sin-2-has-sold-over-500000.html). It hit a million in under three months [DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/news/divinity-original-sin-2-sold-1-million-copies/). By the end of 2017, it had generated $85 million in revenue — the only indie title in the premium PC top 10 [SuperData via WCCFTech](https://wccftech.com/divinity-original-sin-2-85m-revenue-2017/). It earned a 93 on Metacritic [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/divinity-original-sin-ii/), a 99% on OpenCritic [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/3828/divinity-original-sin-2), a GameSpot 10/10 — only the fifteenth game in history to receive that score [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity:_Original_Sin_II) — and a BAFTA for Best Multiplayer [BAFTA/IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt7410340/awards/). PC Gamer named it Game of the Year 2017 [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/game-of-the-year-2017-divinity-original-sin-2/).

But the numbers, as staggering as they are, tell only half the story. Divinity: Original Sin 2 did not merely succeed. It became a *blueprint*. Six years later, Larian released Baldur's Gate 3 — a game built directly on DOS2's foundation, which went on to sell more than 15 million copies and redefine the genre all over again [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/baldurs-gate-3-sales-are-almost-double-of-divinity-original-sin-2). The small Belgian studio had, without quite intending to, drawn the architectural plans for the next decade of computer roleplaying games.

This is the story of how they did it.

---

## 1. The Kickstarter That Changed Everything

The original Divinity: Original Sin had been a modest success — roughly 500,000 copies sold, enough to keep the lights on but not enough to fund the sequel Larian wanted to make. The studio turned to Kickstarter not as a marketing exercise, but as a survival mechanism. They needed to know: was there an audience for a deep, uncompromising, turn-based CRPG in an era dominated by shooters and action games — or had the genre quietly expired while no one was looking?

The answer came in a roar. The $500,000 goal was reached in under twelve hours [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/divinity-original-sin-2-kickstarter-ends-with-quadruple-its-goal/). By the time the campaign ended on September 14, 2015, the total had quadrupled: $2,032,434 from 42,713 backers [Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/larianstudios/divinity-original-sin-2). Every single stretch goal was funded — the turn-based arena mode, the Game Master mode, the Definitive Edition, even an art book and a soundtrack [WCCFTech](https://wccftech.com/divinity-original-sin-2-got-2m-from-kickstarter-all-stretch-goals-unlocked/).

What the backers did not know was that they had just greenlit a production that would push Larian to its breaking point. One studio, forty people — that was the team that had to deliver on those stretch goals. The studio expanded to four locations in a single year. The ambition had outstripped the infrastructure, as ambition so often does, and infrastructure so often resents.

---

## 2. The Chaos of Creation

Swen Vincke stood on stage at GDC 2019 and told the truth about making Divinity: Original Sin 2. It was, by his own admission, a controlled disaster [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025734/The-Making-of-Divinity-Original).

Larian went from one studio to four in twelve months. They hired nine writers for the story — most of whom had never written a game before [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/divinity-original-sin-2/divinity-original-sin-2-story-armour/). The story bible lived in a Google Doc, with all nine writers leaving comments on each other's work. "We were very polite people," Vincke recalled, "and we answered every one of the comments" [RPG Codex](https://rpgcodex.net/article.php?id=11133). One imagines the politeness wavered only when the deadline drew near, but perhaps that is merely cynicism.

The fundamental design constraint — the thing that made DOS2 different from almost every RPG before or since — was a single ruthless rule: *no story armour*. The player could kill any NPC at any time. No essential characters. No invulnerable quest givers. No safety net. The narrative had to survive every contingency — every murder, every theft, every catastrophic failure of player judgment [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/divinity-original-sin-2/divinity-original-sin-2-story-armour/). Most games protect their stories behind glass. Larian threw theirs into the crowd and hoped someone would catch it.

This meant rewrites. Constant, cascading, exhausting rewrites. Scenes had to have backups for their backups. Dialogue trees had to account for branches the writers could not imagine. The rewriting continued until a week before launch [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-we-had-to-scramble-devs-reflect-on-the-making-of-i-divinity-original-sin-2-i-/).

"Original Sin 2 was the first time where we had sufficient resources to do everything well," Vincke said, "and even then we had to scramble" [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-we-had-to-scramble-devs-reflect-on-the-making-of-i-divinity-original-sin-2-i-/).

## 3. The Numbers That Changed the Industry

Then it shipped. And the numbers started coming.

- **500,000** units in the first four days [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/71036-divinity-original-sin-2-has-sold-over-500000.html)
- **1 million** units in two and a half months [DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/news/divinity-original-sin-2-sold-1-million-copies/)
- **$85 million** in revenue in 2017 — #9 among premium PC games that year, the only indie in the top 10 [SuperData via WCCFTech](https://wccftech.com/divinity-original-sin-2-85m-revenue-2017/)

The critical reception was scarcely believable. A **93/100** on Metacritic [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/divinity-original-sin-ii/). A **99%** positive rating on OpenCritic [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/3828/divinity-original-sin-2). **96%** positive on Steam from over 203,000 reviews [SteamSpy](https://www.steamspy.com/app/435150). GameSpot's 10/10 placed DOS2 in a club of roughly fifteen games across the entire history of the medium [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity:_Original_Sin_II). It won a BAFTA for Best Multiplayer [BAFTA/IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt7410340/awards/) and PC Gamer's Game of the Year [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/game-of-the-year-2017-divinity-original-sin-2/).

The game's all-time peak on Steam hit 93,350 concurrent players [SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/435150). Over the life of the title, estimated Steam sales reached roughly 5.5 million copies, grossing approximately $172.9 million on the platform alone [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/435150/Divinity_Original_Sin_2__Definitive_Edition). Total lifetime copies across all platforms are estimated at around 7.5 million [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/baldurs-gate-3-sales-are-almost-double-of-divinity-original-sin-2).

A forty-person studio from Ghent had built one of the most commercially successful and critically acclaimed roleplaying games of all time. The industry had been looking the wrong way, as industries so often do; it was too busy admiring its own reflection to notice the small Belgian studio quietly redrawing the map.

---

## 4. The Blueprint for Baldur's Gate 3

The most important thing Divinity: Original Sin 2 created, however, was not a number. It was a foundation.

Swen Vincke has been explicit about this: "Divinity: Original Sin 2 was the blueprint for Baldur's Gate 3" [INKL/IGN](https://www.inkl.com/news/baldur-s-gate-3-and-original-sin-2-are-the-blueprint-for-divinity-larian-ceo-swen-vincke-on-why-its-next-rpg-is-the-natural-evolution-of-the-studio-s-20-year-journey). The environmental interactions — electrifying water, burning oil, freezing surfaces — the turn-based combat engine, the co-op systems, the layered dialogue trees, the whole reactive architecture of the game world was carried forward and refined [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/how-larian-hopes-to-transcend-baldurs-gate-3s-lega/1100-6474167/).

In fact, Larian had been seeding the connection for years. Divinity: Original Sin 2 contained hidden easter eggs pointing to Baldur's Gate 3 — Mind Maggots, cryptic references to "another world." The hints were so obvious the developers eventually had to water them down [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/rpg/it-was-very-bad-baldurs-gate-3-was-once-heavily-teased-in-larians-divinity-original-sin-2-but-it-was-so-obvious-the-devs-had-to-water-it-down/). The secret was kept poorly, which is to say it was kept perfectly for a studio that wanted its players to feel clever for noticing.

But the relationship between the two games is not a simple story of linear success. Baldur's Gate 3 was almost killed by Divinity: Original Sin 2's success. The team was so exhausted after shipping DOS2 and its Definitive Edition that they could not write the design document for the sequel. Vincke remembered the moment of crisis: "We need to write something, guys. Or we're going to lose this deal" [INKL](https://www.inkl.com/news/the-making-of-baldur-s-gate-3-we-really-wanted-to-make-it-so-that-even-if-you-don-t-know-d-d-or-baldur-s-gate-you-would-still-have-exciting-choices-as-a-player).

They wrote it. They shipped it. Baldur's Gate 3 sold roughly 15 million copies — "almost double" what DOS2 had achieved [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/baldurs-gate-3-sales-are-almost-double-of-divinity-original-sin-2). The blueprint had been stress-tested, validated, and then scaled to a level no one in that Ghent office in 2015 could have reasonably imagined — which is precisely why they were the ones who got to build it.

---

## 5. The Long Tail: A Nine-Year Sales Surge

In January 2026, nine years after its launch, Divinity: Original Sin 2 had its best sales month since 2017 [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/larians-nine-year-old-rpg-has-just-had-its-best-month-since-launch-sales-for-both-bg3-and-d-os-2-have-been-incredible-thanks-to-divinity/). The game hit 27,409 peak concurrent players on Steam [SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/435150). The previous December, after the reveal of the next Divinity game at The Game Awards, average player counts had spiked 170% [SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/435150).

"Sales for both BG3 and D:OS 2 have been incredible since the announcement of Divinity," Vincke confirmed [PC Guide](https://www.pcguide.com/news/swen-vincke-says-baldurs-gate-3-and-original-sin-2-sales-have-been-incredible-since-the-divinity-announcement/). New console releases — PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and Nintendo Switch 2 — extended the game's reach to audiences that had not experienced it the first time [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2026/01/larian-games-doing-better-than-ever-off-the-back-of-divinity-reveal).

The story had circled back on itself. Baldur's Gate 3 — built on DOS2's blueprint — had become a phenomenon. Divinity — the new game announced at The Game Awards — would now build on *both* legacies. DOS2 and BG3 together, Vincke said, "are the blueprint for Divinity" [INKL/IGN](https://www.inkl.com/news/baldur-s-gate-3-and-original-sin-2-are-the-blueprint-for-divinity-larian-ceo-swen-vincke-on-why-its-next-rpg-is-the-natural-evolution-of-the-studio-s-20-year-journey). The architect had become the foundation.

## 6. The Modding Legacy

If the sales figures are the monument, the modding community is the lifeblood that kept the game pulsing for nearly a decade. The Steam Workshop hosts roughly 5,000 items [Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/app/435150/workshop/), while Nexus Mods carries approximately 2,950 mods [Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/divinityoriginalsin2).

The Game Master Mode was the crown jewel — a tool so powerful that players could effectively build their own campaigns inside DOS2's engine. "Build Original Sin 3 if you wanted to," PC Gamer wrote [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/game-of-the-year-2017-divinity-original-sin-2/). Mods like Party Size Evolved and Odinblade's class overhauls became essential additions for returning players, fundamentally reshaping how the game played. The modding tools Larian shipped were not an afterthought; they were a philosophy. Here is the engine. Here are the tools. Make what you want. Most studios treat their players like guests in a well-furnished house. Larian handed them the keys, the blueprints, and a hammer, and trusted they would build something interesting.

That philosophy — trust the player, trust the community — was the same one that had driven the Kickstarter campaign, the no-story-armour design, and the co-operative chaos that made DOS2 unforgettable. It was, in the end, the same bet Larian had always made: that players wanted depth, not shortcuts. That they wanted to break things and be surprised. That they wanted a world that would let them fail, kill, betray, and rewrite the story with every choice. It is an unfashionable belief in an industry that has learned to monetise convenience. It was also entirely correct.

---

The small Belgian studio is not small anymore. Larian now operates across six studios with hundreds of employees. The Kickstarter backers got their game — and then some. The forty people in Ghent became the architects of a genre's renaissance. Divinity: Original Sin 2 was not merely a great RPG. It was the structural template for everything that followed — the hidden foundation beneath Baldur's Gate 3 and the invisible blueprint for whatever Larian builds next.

In 2015, Swen Vincke stood in front of a camera and asked the internet to bet on iteration, on depth, on the belief that players wanted a world without story armour. The internet bet four times what he asked for.

It was the best investment the RPG genre ever made.
