# The Kingdom in Red: How RCB Shed the Nearly-Men Tag and Built a Dynasty

---

**Ahmedabad, 31 May 2026** — The first sign that the Royal Challengers Bengaluru had stopped being themselves arrived not beneath the floodlights of a final, but weeks earlier, on a modest ground in Raipur to which a scheduling misfortune had exiled them. Most title defences splinter under such indignities. RCB, with the perversity of an organisation that has finally learned its lesson, used the inconvenience to forge something harder.

At the Shaheed Veer Narayan Singh International Cricket Stadium on 13 May, with their playoff place still unsecured, Virat Kohli walked out against Kolkata Knight Riders on a pitch that offered the bowlers nothing and produced an unbeaten 105 from 60 deliveries — his fastest IPL hundred by balls faced, and the sort of innings that makes one suspect the 37-year-old has simply decided to ignore the calendar [[ESPNcricinfo pre-final analysis]](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/ipl-2026-can-rcb-make-it-two-in-two-focus-on-virat-kohli-bhuvneshwar-kumar-rajat-patidar-and-others-1538243). That knock carried him past 14,000 T20 runs. More pointedly, it informed every other team in the competition that RCB — the franchise that for eighteen years had been cricket's most elegant unfinished story — had finally acquired a finisher's instinct.

The final confirmation came eighteen days later, under the floodlights of the Narendra Modi Stadium, before more than 100,000 spectators, most of whom had come to will Gujarat Titans toward a second crown. RCB bowled first, held GT to 155 for 8, then chased the target with twelve balls to spare. They won by five wickets. They defended their title. They joined Chennai Super Kings and Mumbai Indians as the only franchises in IPL history to have done so consecutively [[IPLT20 Match Report]](https://www.iplt20.com/news/4377/tata-ipl-2026-final-rcb-v-gt-match-report).

## The 2025 Pivot

To understand how RCB arrived here, one must return to the 2025 mega auction. While other franchises locked away three, four, even five retained players at punishing cost — Mumbai Indians committed nearly ₹75 crore to a handful of stars — RCB adopted a philosophy that looked like frugality and turned out to be foresight [[ESPNcricinfo]](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/ipl-2026-can-rcb-make-it-two-in-two-focus-on-virat-kohli-bhuvneshwar-kumar-rajat-patidar-and-others-1538243). They retained only two established match-winners: Virat Kohli and Rajat Patidar, for a combined ₹32 crore. Yash Dayal was the third and only other retention.

At the time, the strategy was widely read as under-investment. In retrospect, it was the most consequential front-office decision the franchise had ever made — which, for a team famous for making the wrong ones, is saying something.

With a war chest that no other team possessed, RCB spread their budget across the entire squad. They acquired Josh Hazlewood for ₹12.50 crore, Phil Salt for ₹11.50 crore, Jitesh Sharma for ₹11 crore, and Bhuvneshwar Kumar for ₹10.75 crore — four proven performers for the combined price of a single marquee name. They filled the gaps with Krunal Pandya (₹5.75 crore), Tim David (₹3 crore retained), and Devdutt Padikkal (₹2 crore). They had depth at every position. They had answers for every phase of the game [[Wikipedia - 2026 RCB season]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_season).

After the 2024 season — during which Sunrisers Hyderabad had smashed 287 against them — head coach Andy Flower and director of cricket Mike Bobat sat down with Kohli. The message was blunt: the anchor role was no longer enough. Kohli needed to become a different kind of batter. His strike rate jumped from 141 in RCB's first six matches of 2024 to 166 across the remainder of that campaign, and the transformation held [[ESPNcricinfo]](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/ipl-2026-can-rcb-make-it-two-in-two-focus-on-virat-kohli-bhuvneshwar-kumar-rajat-patidar-and-others-1538243).

By 2026, Kohli was striking at 165.84 — his most aggressive IPL season ever — and had become the first batter to score 600-plus runs in four consecutive IPL seasons [[Wikipedia]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_season). He finished the campaign with 675 runs, the fourth-highest in the league [[Cricbuzz Stats]](https://www.cricbuzz.com/cricket-series/9241/indian-premier-league-2026/stats). The man who had built his reputation on accumulation had reinvented himself as a power hitter without losing any of his chase mastery. It is the sort of transformation that happens only when a great player decides that admiration is no longer sufficient.

## The Quiet Captain

Rajat Patidar is not the kind of captain who courts attention. He does not manufacture motivational speeches for the cameras. He does not issue tactical manifestos to the press. He simply makes decisions that keep proving themselves correct.

After RCB's Qualifier 1 demolition of Gujarat Titans in Dharamshala — where Patidar himself struck an unbeaten 93 off 33 balls, the highest score in IPL playoff history — he was asked about his approach. "Just another match," he said, and meant it [[Wikipedia]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_season#Qualifier_1).

That unflappability became RCB's defining characteristic. They finished the league stage at the top of the table with 18 points, their net run rate of +0.783 the best among the three top teams that finished level on points [[Wikipedia]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_season#Points_table). They won six of their seven home games — including the two played in Raipur — while adapting with equanimity to unfamiliar surroundings like Dharamshala, where the Himalayas preside over the boundary rope. They entered the playoffs as favourites, played like favourites, and won like favourites — a sequence that this franchise, for most of its existence, had treated as a contradiction in terms.

Patidar became only the third captain after MS Dhoni and Rohit Sharma to lead a franchise to back-to-back IPL titles [[The Hindu]](https://www.thehindu.com/sport/cricket/ipl-2026-review-rcb-reaps-the-fruits-of-collective-strength-and-clarity-of-roles/article71048468.ece). His numbers — 501 runs at a strike rate of 192.69 — do not tell the full story. The full story is that he built a dressing room where every player knew his role, and where the absence of any single star did not cause the structure to tremble.

## The Final: A Masterclass in Controlled Aggression

The final itself was not a classic. It was a demonstration.

Rajat Patidar won the toss and chose to bowl — a decision that, on a Narendra Modi Stadium pitch that had already beaten RCB once this season (they lost to GT by four wickets there on 30 April), carried genuine risk [[IPLT20 Match Report]](https://www.iplt20.com/news/4377/tata-ipl-2026-final-rcb-v-gt-match-report). Within four overs, the risk had vanished.

Josh Hazlewood, the Bendemeer-born fast bowler who has now won three IPL titles and holds the remarkable record of 10 victories in 10 white-ball finals, removed Shubman Gill, caught behind for 10 [[Cricket.com.au]](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4512147/royal-challengers-bengaluru-beat-gujarat-titans-ipl-2026-final-virat-kohli-75-runs-josh-hazlewood-tim-david-sooryavanshi-mvp). Bhuvneshwar Kumar, the 36-year-old who had once broken RCB hearts in the 2016 final while playing for Sunrisers Hyderabad, dismissed Sai Sudharsan for 12. The short-ball strategy that RCB's seamers had refined over the course of the season was in full effect — six of the eight GT batters fell to short-length deliveries.

Rasikh Salam, the 23-year-old from Jammu & Kashmir whom Krunal Pandya had helped relocate to Baroda for more cricketing opportunities, returned figures of 3 for 27 from his four overs — the best bowling performance of the final [[IPLT20]](https://www.iplt20.com/news/4377/tata-ipl-2026-final-rcb-v-gt-match-report). Krunal himself bowled four overs for just 23 runs and dismissed Jos Buttler with a delivery that the England batter never deciphered. Bhuvneshwar finished with 2 for 29, taking his season tally to 28 wickets — enough to reclaim the Purple Cap from Kagiso Rabada [[ESPNcricinfo live blog]](https://www.espncricinfo.com/series/ipl-2026-1510719/royal-challengers-bengaluru-vs-gujarat-titans-final-1535465/live-match-blog).

Only Washington Sundar, who remained unbeaten on 50 off 37 balls, offered meaningful resistance. When GT finished on 155 for 8, the total felt at least 20 runs short of par — and par, in this RCB side, was a threshold they had long since stopped respecting.

What followed was the fastest team fifty in an IPL final — 3.3 overs. Virat Kohli and Venkatesh Iyer, who had been promoted as an Impact Player, blitzed the Gujarat new-ball attack to all corners. Iyer made 32 off 16 before Mohammed Siraj broke through, but by then the proposition was already settled. The required rate never climbed above six an over.

Kohli reached his half-century in 25 balls — the fastest fifty of his entire IPL career [[BBC Sport]](https://www.bbc.com/sport/cricket/articles/cdxp0pne2nzo). He finished on 75 not out, with nine fours and three sixes, the last of them a straight drive over long-on that sealed the victory and sent 100,000 fans into either silence or ecstasy, depending on which side of the partisan divide they occupied.

## The Spread-Betting Strategy

What distinguished this RCB title from their 2025 breakthrough — and what separates them from the CSK and MI dynasties that preceded them — is the structural philosophy that sustains it.

Where previous RCB teams leaned heavily on two or three glittering names surrounded by thin air, this squad distributed responsibility across eleven or twelve genuine contributors. Phil Salt (463 runs at a strike rate touching 173 in parts of the season) and Devdutt Padikkal (464 runs at a career-best 172) provided top-order firepower. Tim David scored 305 runs at a strike rate of 188, filling the finisher role with clinical regularity. Krunal Pandya contributed 226 runs and 14 wickets, his left-arm spin proving especially troublesome for left-handed batters across the league.

Bhuvneshwar Kumar's 28 wickets led the attack, but he was ably supported by Rasikh Salam (19 wickets), Josh Hazlewood (15 wickets), and Krunal (14 wickets) [[Wikipedia]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_season). Jacob Duffy, the New Zealand quick purchased as cover for Hazlewood, stepped in during the Australian's early-season absence and took Player of the Match in the very first game of the campaign. The squad had redundancy built into every department — the sort of boring, essential infrastructure that never makes a highlights package but wins championships.

You can see the shape of this in the data. RCB's top six run-scorers all struck above 150. Their top five wicket-takers all maintained economies under nine. The burden did not fall on any single individual — and when one player had an off day, another stepped forward without drama or delay.

In Qualifier 1, it was Patidar who produced the defining innings. In the final, it was Kohli. In the league stage, it was Bhuvneshwar (4 for 23 against Mumbai Indians in Raipur), Hazlewood (4 for 12 against Delhi Capitals), and Venkatesh Iyer (73 not out against Punjab Kings) taking turns to be the match-winner [[Wikipedia]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_season#Player_of_the_match).

## What the History Books Will Say

RCB's transformation from perennial nearly-men to back-to-back champions is among the more remarkable arcs in franchise sports history. For seventeen seasons — from 2008 to 2024 — they represented the most expensive kind of failure: a team with cricket's biggest star, a fanbase of devotional intensity, and a consistent inability to finish what they started.

They reached the final three times in that span (2009, 2011, 2016) and lost all three. The 2016 final, in particular, was the kind of heartbreak that defines a franchise — they needed four runs from the last two balls and could not find them. After that loss, Kohli sat in the dressing room for an hour, unable to speak [[BBC Sport]](https://www.bbc.com/sport/cricket/articles/cdxp0pne2nzo).

The shift began in 2025, when a bold captaincy change and a smarter auction strategy ended the eighteen-year drought. But 2026 confirmed what 2025 had only suggested: that RCB are no longer a team that hopes to win. They are a team that expects to — and expectation, in a tournament as capricious as the IPL, is the harder attitude to earn.

They enter the 2027 season with their core intact, their tactical identity established, and the knowledge that only one team in IPL history — Mumbai Indians, 2019–20 — has ever completed a three-peat. If RCB are to join that conversation, they will do so with a model that other franchises are already beginning to study: spread your budget, build depth, define roles, and trust your process. It is the kind of formula that sounds simple and turns out to be almost impossible — which is precisely why it took eighteen years to discover.
